Cuewand design rules
====================

For anyone changing remote.html (the phone) or host.html (the pairing page).
Both pages use the same tokens, by the same names, in their :root blocks.


Rules
-----
1. One primary action per screen. On the talk view it is Next.
2. Nothing moves under the thumb. The view never changes by itself; new
   buttons that appear where a finger may be ignore it for about a second;
   a turn of the phone changes the layout only after 0.7 s, never while a
   finger is down, and Back/Next ignore presses for a second after.
3. Every outcome is named where the thumb is: on the Next button, with its
   own shape (ring, inset ring, dashed frame, amber bars, red cross) and,
   on Android, its own vibration. Messages about a press sit just above
   Back/Next, never at the top of the screen.
4. The time never leaves the Next button. When the big line shows a status,
   the bottom line shows "12:30 left".
5. Colour is never the only signal. Red means over time or trouble; amber
   means look here. Nothing else is red or amber.
6. Anything that throws work away asks for a second tap, and offers Undo
   for ten seconds when it can be undone.
7. Nothing is claimed before the computer confirms it.
8. One number per fact. The time left is on Next and nowhere else on the
   talk view; other lines say what to do ("Results · move on").
9. When room runs short, words give way in a fixed order and nothing is
   cut mid-word: the step dots, then "of" ("7 /26"), then the top
   buttons' labels (they come back as soon as they fit); on Next the
   word "Next" when the time would otherwise shrink below 85 %, then the
   size of the big line (never below what fits). In the notes, the step
   you are on keeps two clear lines: "Next click" goes to one line, then
   steps aside, then the notes shrink (not below 60 %). Tests check
   every layout at 90-200 %.
10. Nothing pops up near Back/Next during a show that the presenter did
   not cause. Questions that can wait (a phone's name) live in the Menu.

Logo
----
The wand (the phone in your hand) points at the cue, the product's own
Next button: a small squircle with a chevron. One axis runs from the
grip through the tip to the cue's centre.
  --brand #0F6E7A       the tile (3.5:1 on black, 6.0:1 on white)
  --brand-wand #C4E8EC  the wand (4.6:1 on the tile)
  --brand-cue #FFFFFF   the cue, the brightest part (6.0:1 on the tile)
The word "cuewand" is the page's text colour (#EDEFF3 on dark,
#0A0D13 on light). The three --brand colours belong to the logo only and
never carry a meaning in the interface, so amber still means only "look
here" and the logo never competes with a warning.
In the pages: one inline symbol (#cw-logo), used where class="mark";
30 px tall on the pairing page, 22 px on the phone, 24 px above the
heading, at the top of every full screen except the start screen, where
it is centred with the rest. Never on the talk view, never a button.
The chevron is its own path (.cw-chev), shown only at 1.5 device pixels
per CSS pixel or more; at 16 and 24 px (favicon, small icons) the cue is
plain. Files: logo/ (lockups for dark and light, icons, favicon.ico,
apple-touch-icon.png, one-colour versions). The server serves the
browser and home-screen icons from there.


The Next button
---------------
Its fill level is the time left; its colour is how worried to be. The
digits are drawn twice and clipped at the level line: light above the
line, dark ink over the fill, so they read at 7:1 or more wherever the
line crosses them.
  calm      level on level-empty; text / level-ink
  warn      attention on attention-deep; attention / attention-ink
  over      danger-deep, no level; danger-text, danger outline
  paused    as calm or warn, with a framed "Paused"; nothing dimmed
  trouble   danger-deep, danger-text; dashed outline when not connected
  following another phone   a 10 px hatched inset edge; clock untouched
An amber notch (14 px) at both edges marks where the level should be when
the current PowerPoint section ends (from the rehearsal).
When the time would have to shrink below 85 % to fit, the plain word
"Next" is dropped instead; a status ("Sending", "Last slide") keeps its
bottom line. The big line steps down (--fit) until it is inside the
button and clear of the bottom line. Press shapes sit behind the words, never over them.
Automated tests check digits >= 7:1 and labels >= 4.5:1 in every state.


Components
----------
big button (primary white / secondary outline / danger), line row (with
an optional explanation, which is the control's description), chip,
switch, sheet (bottom sheet on phones, centred panel on tablets), mode
band (rehearsing / following another phone; one at a time), the strip
above Back/Next (messages and Undo; exactly as wide as Back/Next, in every
layout), toast (top, for things not about a press), full screens
(connect, Setup, ready check) with their button in a footer that never
moves and "more below" just above that footer. On the talk view, more
notes below is a small arrow at the right edge, off the words; the fade
is min(54 px, 20 %) of the notes area.
Back to N    only on a hidden backup slide reached by a jump; amber edge;
             the Slides view's Back says the same. Ends when a press lands
             on an ordinary slide.
Slide grid   by section; on wide screens one even grid, the section's
             name on its first slide (headings stay for screen readers).


Layouts
-------
phone       portrait, shortest side < 600
land        landscape, height < 600: picture column left, notes and
            Back/Next right (Back/Next at most 45 %)
tab         portrait, shortest side >= 600: slide and next slide side by
            side, Next at most 520 px and Back at most 200 px, on the
            hand's side
desk        landscape, height >= 600: presenter view
